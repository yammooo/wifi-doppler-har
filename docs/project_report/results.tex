\section{Experimental Evaluation and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Protocol and Metrics}

The frozen classifier is the reproduced SHARP network trained on official
S1a/S1b/S1c maps from the \(0\)--\(60\%\) recording interval and selected on
\(60\)--\(80\%\). No classifier weights are updated. Final evaluation uses the
same scenarios over the disjoint \(90\)--\(100\%\) interval, with stride 30 and
718 windows from the five classifier activities. Each method receives
identical raw windows. The recording-mean centering expected by the classifier
is estimated independently for every representation and does not use labels.

We report full-map and off-center MSE against the official SHARP target. A frame
is active when its maximum outside bins 45--55 exceeds 0.2; precision, recall,
and F1 compare predicted and target support. The motion-mass ratio is total
predicted divided by target off-center excess power, with one ideal. Frozen
classifier accuracy is the primary task metric. Loading the targets through
the prepared-data path reproduces all 718 direct SHARP predictions, confirming
that map and classifier provenance are aligned.

\subsection{RQ1: Can the Student Learn the Mapping?}

\textbf{Memorization sanity check.}
Before full-data training, two students were optimized repeatedly on the same
16 motion-rich PI windows. With 30 subcarriers, run \texttt{cwj0myug} reached
\(3.881\times10^{-5}\) MSE; with all 242, \texttt{tm8nyt19} reached
\(4.194\times10^{-5}\). Both reproduced the selected peaks. Thus, the
implementation is differentiable and has sufficient capacity to memorize the
local transform, although this test says nothing about generalization.

\textbf{Matched full-data behavior.}
With official SHARP supervision, run \texttt{az9k6ori} behaves very differently
from the preliminary students. Motion-aware training loss decreases from
0.03397 to 0.01264 over all 50 epochs. All-scenario validation decreases from
0.02800 to 0.01415, while S1a/S1b/S1c validation decreases from 0.02506 to
0.01326 and reaches its minimum, 0.01212, at epoch 45. There is no widening
train--validation gap. Because training is motion-balanced while validation is
natural, their absolute values are not directly comparable, but their joint
late improvement is evidence against both early collapse and classic
overfitting.

The held-out example in \figref{fig:qualitative} confirms genuine conditional
learning. The student places off-center responses at the target event times and
on the correct side of the spectrum. Its main error is now spatial smoothing:
thin target peaks become wider lobes, and nearby temporal events merge. The
full-resolution frequency-aware decoder therefore removes the center-only
shortcut, but does not preserve all fine motion geometry.

\subsection{RQ2: Do Reconstruction Metrics Predict HAR Utility?}

\tabref{tab:main-results} gives the matched comparison. The full-resolution
student reaches \(63.09\%\), a 19.78-point improvement over the coarse
spatial-head model and well above raw STFT. Its 95\% Wilson interval is
\(59.50\)--\(66.54\%\), disjoint from the smaller model's
\(39.74\)--\(46.97\%\). The architectural improvement is therefore reflected
in both map and downstream metrics.

Aggregate improvement does not imply that the neural representation is
classifier-equivalent. The full model is nearly perfect for empty-room and
still cases (\(100\%\) for E and \(99.36\%\) for L) and reaches \(91.67\%\) on
running, but only \(1.30\%\) on walking and \(1.98\%\) on jumping. Specifically,
152 of 154 walking windows and 92 of 101 jumping windows are classified as
running. Its balanced accuracy is therefore \(58.86\%\), below its aggregate
\(63.09\%\). The student has learned whether and approximately where motion
occurs, as supported by F1 \(0.758\) and mass ratio 1.21, but smoothing removes
the spectral and temporal details needed to distinguish dynamic activities.

In this provenance-matched experiment, off-center MSE and support F1 broadly
rank the methods in the same order as classifier accuracy. They remain
insufficient by themselves: active support treats a broad running-like lobe as
successful even when it erases class-discriminative shape. Reconstruction and
task metrics must therefore be reported together.

\subsection{RQ3: What Does Explicit Phase Correction Reveal?}

The affine baseline unwraps each antenna's subcarrier phase, fits the
packet-wise phase difference from the first packet as
\(\alpha_t s+\beta_t\) by least squares, subtracts it, and applies the fixed
Hann/STFT tail. It is not proposed as a novel algorithm. Nevertheless, it
matches SHARP at \(91.36\%\) accuracy; both share the Wilson interval
\(89.08\)--\(93.21\%\). It agrees with SHARP's decisions on \(98.33\%\) of
windows and obtains off-center MSE \(1.55\times10^{-4}\), F1 \(0.943\), and
motion-mass ratio 0.982.

This establishes that the local raw windows contain enough information for a
high-utility representation. It also isolates the main remaining neural error:
the direct student learns substantial phase robustness and event localization,
but its generic decoder approximates sharp sparse structure by conditional
averages. Retaining an explicit nuisance-phase model is both more accurate and
easier to justify than increasing CNN capacity again.

\subsection{Limitations and Answer to the RQ}

The experiments use one seed, one frozen classifier, and 92 aligned legacy AR
recordings; five candidate pairs are excluded for temporal mismatch. The
classifier test measures compatibility with SHARP's established interface, not
the performance of a newly trained classifier adapted to student maps. No
equivalent end-to-end runtime comparison is available.

The answer to the primary question is therefore mixed but operationally
negative. Direct regression can learn a meaningful approximation: the corrected
model generalizes event support and reaches \(63.09\%\), so the task is not
infeasible. It does not preserve enough fine structure to replace SHARP at
\(91.36\%\), especially for walking and jumping. A phase-aware hybrid that
sanitizes CSI explicitly and learns only a residual or downstream transform is
the most justified next design.
