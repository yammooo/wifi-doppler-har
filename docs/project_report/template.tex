\title{Approximating SHARP Doppler Maps from Raw Wi-Fi CSI:\\
A Direct Learning Approach}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Gianmaria Frigo, Mattia Pinton}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Department of Information Engineering\\
University of Padova, Italy}
}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Commodity Wi-Fi channel state information (CSI) enables privacy-preserving human activity recognition, but robust Doppler maps require careful phase sanitization.
We study whether an end-to-end network can replace the optimization-intensive front end of SHARP by regressing its Doppler maps directly from short raw-CSI windows.
We build a reproducible teacher--student pipeline and progressively correct antenna coupling, subcarrier processing, temporal alignment, spectral resolution, motion imbalance, and loss design.
The final 1.314-million-parameter model uses shared antenna weights, a frequency--time encoder, and a full-resolution two-dimensional decoder.
Reconstruction fidelity is evaluated separately from task utility with a frozen SHARP classifier.
When trained against the official precomputed SHARP Doppler traces, the full-resolution
student localizes held-out motion and reaches \(63.09\%\) frozen-classifier
accuracy, versus \(43.31\%\) for the antenna-shared temporal model with a coarse
spectral head, but it smooths spectral detail and confuses walking and jumping with running. The
official Doppler representation reaches \(91.36\%\). A non-learned,
SHARP-derived affine sanitization baseline followed by the fixed Doppler
transform also reaches \(91.36\%\), with \(94.3\%\)
active-frame F1 versus \(75.8\%\) for the best student. Direct regression
therefore learns part of the transformation but is not a drop-in SHARP
replacement. The results favor phase-aware hybrid models; the experiments do
not isolate the contribution of every architecture and training change.
\end{abstract}
