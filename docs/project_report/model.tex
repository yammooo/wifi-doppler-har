\section{Problem Formulation and Data Pipeline}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Supervised Reconstruction Task}

For each local window, the input is raw CSI
\(\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{A\times S\times T_x}\), with \(A=4\) receiver
streams, \(S=242\) usable subcarriers, and \(T_x=370\) packets. Real and
imaginary components are exposed as separate numeric channels, so a batch has
shape
\[
    [B,4,242,370,2].
\]
The target \(\mathbf{Y}\in[\floorval,1]^{B\times4\times340\times100}\)
contains one normalized 100-bin Doppler spectrum per antenna and output time.
A 31-packet analysis window requires 15 packets of context on either side, so
370 packets align exactly with 340 valid target frames. The objective is to
learn \(f_\theta(\mathbf{X})\approx\mathbf{Y}\) without exposing activity
labels to the student.

\subsection{Teacher and Reference Distributions}

The distributed targets are the \emph{official precomputed SHARP Doppler
traces}: the unmodified per-recording outputs distributed with the original
dataset. Each trace contains, for every receiver stream and time frame, a
normalized 100-bin Doppler spectrum. A 340-frame crop therefore forms the
target array \([4,340,100]\), which we call a Doppler map: ``map'' denotes this
two-dimensional time--Doppler representation, not the learned
raw-CSI-to-Doppler function. The traces follow the SHARP chain
\cite{meneghello2023sharp}: packet-level normalization, sparse path estimation,
selection of the strongest path as phase reference, affine phase sanitization,
Hann-windowed Doppler analysis, per-frame normalization, and flooring at
\(\floorval\approx0.0631\). We pair these traces with their raw CSI. The student
is trained and evaluated against windows cropped from the traces, while the
frozen HAR classifier from the reproduction notebook was trained on windows
from the same traces. This avoids comparing student outputs with a classifier
trained on a separately regenerated Doppler representation.

The final experiment uses 92 temporally aligned recordings from all 12
available AR scenario folders represented in the official release
\cite{meneghello2023dataset}. Five of 97 candidate pairs are excluded because
their raw and Doppler lengths do not have SHARP's expected temporal offset;
forcing them into training would misalign input and target frames. Within every
remaining recording, the student uses \(0\)--\(80\%\) for training,
\(80\)--\(90\%\) for validation, and \(90\)--\(100\%\) for final testing. All
12 scenarios contribute to training and to an all-scenario validation diagnostic.
Early stopping uses S1a/S1b/S1c because the frozen downstream classifier
evaluation is defined on those scenarios. The classifier itself was trained on \(0\)--\(60\%\), selected on
\(60\)--\(80\%\), and is evaluated on the same disjoint \(90\)--\(100\%\)
interval as the student. This tests unseen temporal portions of known
recordings, not unseen recordings, people, or environments. A 31-frame guard
between intervals prevents context leakage. Training windows use stride 170,
while frozen-classifier evaluation uses stride 30, reproducing exactly the SHARP implementation.

\section{Learning Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Architecture Progression}

We report three milestones, each responding to a failure of the preceding
design. The initial \emph{flattened temporal U-Net} applies Conv1D over time
after folding antennas, subcarriers, and complex components into channels. Its
400 output channels have no Doppler-neighborhood structure, making the observed
per-bin mean spectrum and vertical ridge easy to learn. The
\emph{antenna-shared temporal U-Net with coarse spectral head} uses one
estimator for every receiver stream, preventing antenna-specific shortcuts, and
adds 2-D convolutions across neighboring time--Doppler pixels. However, it
mixes subcarriers in the first projection and interpolates 25 predicted bins to
100, a low-resolution bottleneck that favors broad peaks.

The final \emph{antenna-shared frequency--time U-Net with full-resolution
decoder} retains ordered subcarriers through a 2-D encoder because SHARP
exploits cross-subcarrier phase structure. It uses GroupNorm instead of
batch-composition-dependent BatchNorm, predicts all 100 bins directly, and
crops 15 frames per side rather than resizing 370 inputs to 340 outputs, thus
preserving the timing of 31-packet Doppler windows.

These are the initial design, the evaluated intermediate design, and the final
corrected design. Other runs varied training, data, or capacity, or served as
diagnostics rather than introducing a distinct architecture. \tabref{tab:models} summarises these milestones.

\subsection{Antenna-Shared Frequency--Time U-Net}

The final architecture processes antennas independently with identical weights.
It reshapes
\[
[B,A,S,T,2]\rightarrow[BA,2,S,T],
\]
so antenna identity cannot become a shortcut, but every antenna--window pair is
retained. A \(9\times7\) frequency--time stem and two strided 2-D stages produce
feature tensors of sizes
\[
\begin{split}
[BA,64,61,370]&\rightarrow[BA,96,31,185]\\
&\rightarrow[BA,128,16,92].
\end{split}
\]
Residual blocks use GroupNorm, avoiding recording-composition-dependent
BatchNorm statistics. Only after these convolutions have modeled local
subcarrier structure is the remaining frequency axis averaged; a standard
\(1\times1\) projection then mixes latent channels.

The bottleneck is projected directly to
\([BA,16,92,100]\). A proper 2-D time--Doppler decoder upsamples time through
92, 185, and 370 positions and merges two projected encoder skips. It predicts
all 100 Doppler bins directly. The
output head produces \([BA,370,100]\); removing exactly 15 frames from each
side yields the valid \([B,A,340,100]\) output. \figref{fig:architecture}
summarizes this flow. The model has 1,314,385 trainable parameters. These
changes correct the identified decoder and alignment flaws.

\subsection{Motion-Aware Objective and Sampling}

Most target energy lies near zero Doppler, so plain MSE rewards a stationary
center ridge. We use
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{map}}
 +0.25\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{motion}}
 +0.25\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{leak}}
 +0.05\mathcal{L}_{W}.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
\(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{map}}\) is MSE over all antennas, times, and 100 bins.
Bins 45--55 are designated stationary. Outside them,
\(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{motion}}\) weights squared error continuously by target
activity defined as:
\[
    w_{\mathrm{motion}} = \operatorname{clip}\left(\frac{Y-\floorval}{1-\floorval},0,1\right).
\]
\(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{leak}}\) is the mean linear excess above the background
floor on pixels where the target is at the floor. Finally,
\(\mathcal{L}_{W}\) is a one-dimensional Wasserstein term  that accounts for distance between Doppler bins, so predicting a peak near the correct location is penalized less than placing the same amount of power far away.
It normalizes positive
off-center power into a distribution and averages the absolute difference
between predicted and target cumulative sums along the 100-bin Doppler axis.

Training uses deterministic motion stratification. Within each recording, the
top quartile of windows by active off-center frame fraction is marked
motion-rich. Every epoch includes all 1,748 rich windows and an equal-size,
non-duplicated rotating subset of ordinary windows. Batches contain 16 rich and
16 ordinary windows drawn across eight recordings. Validation and test remain
unmodified. The optimizer is Adam with learning rate \(10^{-3}\), automatic
mixed precision, a 50-epoch limit, and patience 8 on S1a/S1b/S1c validation
loss. Training completed all 50 epochs and restored the best checkpoint from
epoch 45. The smaller comparison model uses all stride-30 windows and batch
size 256.
